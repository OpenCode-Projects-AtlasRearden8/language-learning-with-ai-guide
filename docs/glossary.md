# Glossary

Every term used in this guide, in zero-jargon plain English. Skim it once
now and again whenever a word lands funny. (For general AI and study-skill
terms not covered here — model, hallucination, spaced review math — see
the sister guide's glossary.)

**A1 / A2 / B1** — The common worldwide shorthand for beginner levels
(A1 = first words and set phrases, A2 = handle everyday exchanges alone,
B1 = describe experiences and explain opinions). When a chapter says
"know your level roughly," A1/A2/B1 is enough precision.

**AI partner** — The AI configured as a conversation partner, per the
session template in chapter 2. Not "AI in general" — a particular setup
with rules: language of use, no English unless asked, corrections table
at the end.

**Box** — The strength level of a vocabulary word (0–7) in `vocab.csv`.
Box 0 means brand new; higher boxes mean you've recalled it successfully
more times, with longer gaps between reviews.

**Correction table** — The table your AI partner gives at the end of a
session: what you said | a more natural way | why. The single most
valuable artifact of each session; you harvest one line from it per day.

**Corrections-table harvest** — The daily two-second act of picking **one**
line from the correction table and adding it to your phrase list or
vocab file. One is deliberate; five is clutter.

**Due date** — The `due` column: the next day a word should appear in
review. Managed automatically by `vocab.py`.

**Fossilization** — When a mistake becomes permanent because nobody
corrected it often enough to matter. This is why the correction table
ends every session, and why chapter 5 insists you verify rules against
real sources before you rely on them.

**Input** — Language you consume (read, hear). Chapter 6 is a catalogue
of input sources that already exist in your life: films, songs, labels.

**Intercepted media** — Real-world text in your target language that
enters your day almost by accident: an import label, a bilingual street
sign, a song lyric. Cheap immersion, because you didn't schedule it.

**Local model** — An AI that runs entirely on your own computer (for
example via Ollama). Free to use, private, works offline. Chapter 2,
Option A.

**Minimum viable session** — The smallest session in the world: review
the cards due today, close the file. For bad days; keeps the habit from
cooling off without inflating it.

**Phrase list** — A plain markdown file of full sentences for one real
situation, in the format from `templates/phrase-list-template.md`.
Sentences you've *stolen* — from drills, films, songs, labels.

**Plateau** — The stretch (often weeks 8–16) when measurable progress
goes quiet while underlying reorganization continues. Protocol for it:
chapter 8.

**Register** — The "flavor" of speech for a setting: formal clinic
speech, market banter, children's story, argument about dogs vs. cats.
Different registers need different words; rotating registers (chapter 8,
week 2) is a plateau-buster.

**Rescue phrase** — A short, pre-memorized line that restarts a stalled
conversation: "Sorry, slower please" / "I have limited [LANGUAGE], do
you speak English?" Chapter 7 makes these the one thing you carry in
your pocket.

**Session template** — The four-line message you open every practice
session with (chapter 2), which turns a general AI chat into a patient
practice partner.

**Shadowing** — Repeating a native audio line immediately after (or
under) the speaker, copying rhythm as much as words. The two-minute
optional step in chapter 6's film protocol.

**Spaced review** — Meeting a word at increasing intervals (tomorrow,
then two days, four, eight...) instead of cramming. Implemented in
`vocab.py` with plain boxes; the reasoning behind it is in the sister
guide.

**Streak (the good kind)** — Not a computer counter: the quiet sense of
"I am a person who practices this language." That is what the minimum
viable session protects.

**Weekly plan card** — One page for one week: the focus (words /
sentences / listening), twelve or so checkboxes, nothing else. Chapter 1;
the cards themselves live in `templates/weekly-plan-cards.md`.

**Word haul** — What you add at the end of the close (chapter 1): 2–3
new words or one phrase met that day. The vocabulary of your own life,
delivered by your own days.
