# Phrase List Templates

Every phrase list in this guide uses the same small format. Keep each
list about **one real situation** — that's what makes it findable a
month later. Copy the skeleton below for each situation, or copy the
filled example to see how it looks in practice.

## The skeleton

```markdown
# Situation — [name it: "Bakery", "Clinic intake", "Small talk at pickup"]

When: [when does this happen in MY life]
Version: v1 — [date]
Source notes: [drill? film? overheard? nothing yet]

## Must-know (say or hear these; practice out loud)

- [phrase in language] — [plain meaning] — [how I'd reply]
- [phrase in language] — [plain meaning] — [how I'd reply]

## Rescue phrases (the conversation restarters)

- [phrase] — [meaning]

## Word-thieves (things I learned by accident, keep for later)

- [word I met] — [what I think it means] — [where I met it]

## My line (one sentence of MY OWN to nail this situation)

- [sentence in language] — [what it means, my words]
```

## Rules of the format (why it looks like this)

- **One situation per file.** A list covering "all of travel" is
  unusable in the moment; a list covering "the taxi" is gold.
- **Every entry has a reply slot.** Phrases in real conversations come
  in pairs; the "how I'd reply" half is what turns a phrase list into a
  rehearsal.
- **Rescue phrases at the top level of every list** — when you're
  stalling, the list should have its own life raft.
- **"Word-thieves" not "sorry I don't remember"**: words you picked up
  by accident get their own shelf until you graduate them into
  `vocab.csv`.
- **"My line"** — one sentence you built, verified against chapter 5's
  routine if you're unsure about its grammar. This is the sentence you
  will actually deliver in real life.
- **Version lines, not deletion.** When you learn a better way to say
  something, keep the old line with a strikethrough and a new line
  under it — your own progress record, free.

## Getting the starter contents from your partner

When a new situation is empty, fill it in one session:

> Fill my phrase list for [situation]. Give 8 phrases I'd really say or
> hear, most common first: natural [LANGUAGE] version, plain meaning,
> what to listen for in the reply. Then verify: which of these would a
> printed phrasebook actually list for [city/country]? Correct yours if
> not.

(Pair this with chapter 5's check routine if the partner states any
grammar rule while explaining.)

## A filled example (first two entries shown)

```markdown
# Situation — Pharmacy

When: any trip over 5 days; I always need something small
Version: v1 — 2026-10-30
Source notes: Drill 3, session 2026-10-30

## Must-know

- ¿Tiene algo para el dolor de cabeza? — Do you have something for a
  headache? — (reply slot: "sí/no + price or brand")
- Necesito algo para la piel irritada. — I need something for irritated
  skin. — (listen for: "¿lea o crema?" — spray or cream)
```

**Remember the split:** words go in `vocab.csv` (chapter 4's tool),
sentences stay here in phrase files. The tool schedules words; this
file rehearses speech.
